Prior-Authorization Workflow: AS-IS/TO-BE with AI-Assisted Triage

A full process-redesign case study — swimlane mapping, gap analysis, BRD, FRD, and a UAT plan — for the workflow clinical staff say costs them the most administrative time.

The business problem

Prior authorization is one of the most cited sources of administrative burden in outpatient care. Public payer disclosures now required under the 2026 CMS-0057-F rule show a mean standard-determination turnaround of 2.8 days — but the more telling number is the appeal reversal rate: over half of denials get overturned on appeal, which points to a front-end submission process that's generating avoidable denials, not just slow ones. The business question isn't "how do we get payers to move faster" — it's "how much of our own 2.8-day-plus cycle is actually our process, and where does it break."

Gap analysis

The bottleneck isn't the payer's decision window — it's the document-gathering step inside the PA Specialist lane, where incomplete submissions trigger the "queue" box shown above: additional-documentation requests that reset the clock. This matches the industry pattern, where missing documentation is consistently cited as the single biggest driver of extended timelines, adding well beyond the standard window when it happens.

BA artifacts produced

BRDProblem: Incomplete initial submissions drive avoidable denials and re-work cycles. Success metrics: reduce first-pass denial rate below the 11.5% cross-plan average; cut submission-to-decision cycle time; reduce PA Specialist hours spent on document chase-down.
FRDSystem shall pre-screen intake documentation against payer-specific requirement checklists before submission, using an AI-assisted triage step to flag missing fields and predict likely denial reasons prior to send.
TO-BE stepAI-assisted intake triage sits between "gathers docs" and "submits" — it checks the packet against a payer/procedure-specific checklist and flags gaps back to the PA Specialist before the clock starts, instead of after a denial.
Acceptance criteriaGiven an incomplete submission packet, when triage runs, then the system identifies at least the missing fields a human reviewer would catch, and does not block a complete packet from submission (no false-positive holds > 2%).
UAT planTest against a sample of historical denied requests: does the triage step flag the same documentation gap that caused the original denial? Test against a sample of clean approvals: does triage let them through without unnecessary delay?
