Claims-Status Chatbot: A Full Requirements & Risk Package

A BRD/FRD for an AI feature, written the way a BA should when the feature is AI-powered — with acceptance criteria, a UAT plan, and a dedicated risk, bias, and privacy section before a line of code is written.

RoleBusiness Analyst
Feature typeAI Claims-Status Chatbot
DeliverableRequirements doc, not an app
ArtifactsBRD · FRD · UAT · Risk Register

The business problem

Members call a health plan's support line most often to ask one question: "what's happening with my claim?" That's a high-volume, low-complexity query that ties up live agents who should be handling the calls that actually need judgment. An AI chatbot can answer it — but the moment it's wrong, it's wrong in a way that touches someone's medical bill, which means the requirements doc has to do more work than a typical feature spec.

BRD

Business objectiveDeflect claims-status inquiries from live agents to a self-service AI assistant, reducing average call handle time and hold queue length without reducing member satisfaction with claim communication.
Success metrics% of status inquiries fully resolved without agent escalation; member satisfaction score on chatbot-resolved sessions vs. agent-resolved; zero incidents of incorrect claim status or amount communicated to a member.
In / out of scopeIn scope: claim status, expected processing time, what documentation is outstanding. Out of scope: appeals, benefit interpretation, anything requiring clinical judgment — those route to a live agent by design, not as a fallback.

FRD & acceptance criteria

Functional requirementSystem shall retrieve claim status directly from the claims-processing system of record and present it verbatim; system shall not generate, infer, or paraphrase a status, amount, or date that is not present in the source record.
Acceptance criteriaGiven a claim ID with a known status, when a member asks about it, then the chatbot returns the exact status and date from the source system. Given an ambiguous or multi-claim query, when the system cannot resolve a single claim with confidence, then it asks a clarifying question rather than guessing.
Escalation requirementSystem shall detect out-of-scope requests (appeals, benefit disputes, clinical questions) and route to a live agent with conversation context, rather than attempting to answer.

Risk, bias & privacy section

Hallucination riskHighest-severity risk: the assistant states an incorrect claim status or dollar amount. Mitigation: retrieval-only design (no generated numbers), plus a confidence threshold below which the system defers to a human instead of answering.
Bias riskEscalation-routing logic must be tested across member language preference and communication channel to confirm non-English or lower-literacy members aren't disproportionately deflected to self-service without an easy path to a live agent.
PrivacyChatbot session logs contain PHI by nature of the conversation; requires the same retention, access-control, and audit-log standards as a live agent call, not a lighter standard because it's "just a bot."
AccountabilityEvery chatbot-resolved session is logged with the source-system data it displayed, so a disputed conversation can be reconstructed and audited — same standard applied to a recorded agent call.

UAT plan

Test set built from three categories: (1) clean claim-status lookups with a known correct answer, verifying zero fabricated details; (2) ambiguous/multi-claim queries, verifying the system asks rather than guesses; (3) out-of-scope requests (appeals, clinical questions), verifying correct escalation with context preserved for the agent. Pass criteria: 100% accuracy on category 1 status/amount fields — this is the one category where "close enough" isn't an acceptable bar.

Delivered as a requirements and risk package, in line with how AI features are actually scoped before build in regulated environments — no chatbot was built for this case study.
